# Personas

The home of the operator's AI agents: each with its own name, picture, personality and memory, taking part in the operator's apps the way a person does.

For the operator, who gives each agent its name and rights, and for the people who meet his agents in his apps.

## What runs now

The runner. Each agent signs in to the realm as itself, with its own client, and for every app it may reach that listens it keeps one subscription open on the app's MCP endpoint and reads its standing whenever the app says something changed. When it is invited to a call it accepts, and it appears there under the name the realm gives it, with the AI mark. It never leaves on its own; it is removed with its seat, its lease or its client. In a call it reads every line from its join on, takes what was said before a restart or during a pause as read, and answers once a line said after names it, as a whole word in any case, with one model turn through the operator's LiteLLM gateway; it never takes its own line as addressed to it. With a `[memory]` table it keeps every line it reads, verbatim, in a Postgres of its own, and when he is addressed he is also told what was said in his other visits of the day: back in a call four hours after he left it, he knows what was said there.

In an app that declares `speak`, an agent whose persona file has a `voice` section (`voice: {windowBytes: <bytes>}`, the most of the conversation one decision sends) hears and speaks in its calls through the agents library's voice loop, and a place of that app runs no turn per addressed line. One voice loop runs per place and window generation; it ends when its place is removed, paused or invited again, when the window's generation changes and when the connection is lost, and never when a stream ends at its bound. It hears every line from the agent's join on, written or spoken (what was said before a restart or during a pause is taken as read), and wakes on a line that names the agent as a whole word, on a question (a line ending in `?`) and on the first line after its answer, which a written place does not; a line that does not wake it is still part of what it knows of the call. One decision runs at a time, and its answer is spoken sentence by sentence through the app's `speak`, each after the one before has played for the length the app says, to at most 30 seconds. A refused sentence ends the answer and is logged as `speak_refused` with the app's code; a decision that ends short is logged as `turn_ended` with its code and is not retried; when it ended before any sentence played, its lines are left for the next line that wakes the agent, and when a sentence had played they count as answered. A voice decision is offered no tools, and `speak` is the voice's own: naming it in an app's `tools` is refused at start. What the loop hears is kept like what a turn reads, and with `[memory]` the loop is told the day once, when it starts, in the shape a turn is told it; every decision sends it again, so `today_bytes` bounds it there too.

A place whose state is no conversation, a table for instance, is served through a view.
The app sets `serves_view` on the agent's standing there, whatever the place's kind, and the agent reads that place through its view and never through a window.
The view holds a `revision`, whether the place waits for him, and the app's own `state`.
An invitation to such a place is accepted like a call's, but only in an app where the agent can take turns (a persona, the gateway and bound tools), because a seated agent that cannot act leaves the place waiting.
Only apps the operator configured to listen can invite the agent, and what people put into a view reaches him as their lines do.
After every read of the standing, and while no turn runs, the agent reads the views of his active view places in the standing's order.
The first that waits for him gets one model turn.
The turn is told one JSON message: the app (its `mcp_url`), the place's kind and id, his name, that the place waits for him, the state as the app sent it, and his history: every line he took in at any of his places since the runner started, in the order he took it, each with its app and place and his own lines marked, within `history_bytes`; past it the oldest are left out and the turn is told that they were.
A line is part of his history whether or not it names him, and what he took from a place stays in it when he leaves the place.
It has no instruction; what to do at a table is the persona's to say.
The turn holds the bound tools of its app and of the tools-only apps, and it ends with its place, as a written turn does.
It reads no day and stores nothing.
A waiting revision gets one turn, and no second one at that revision, however the turn ended: with a tool call that moves nothing, with no call, with a refused call or with a failed relay.
The runner does not ask again by itself; an app that wants him to act again moves the revision.
A tool's error result reaches the model inside the same turn.
The agent then waits for the app's next revision.
An app therefore moves the revision with every answer of the agent, a declined word included.
The state reaches no log line.

Which apps an agent can use is the realm's group membership, not this configuration. When the realm or an app refuses an agent (a disabled client, a missing group), that agent's loop for that app is suspended and logged once, and it resumes when the refusal ends, without a restart.

## Run it

Install with `uv sync`, point `PERSONAS_CONFIG` at a TOML file and start `personas`. The file holds the realm's token endpoint, the absolute path `ready_file`, the apps (MCP URL and scope) and the agents (client id and the path of the file holding its client secret). An app that lets its agents answer also lists the tools their turns may call as `tools`, with `max_tool_calls`, the most calls one turn makes in that app; the two are set together. A call past an app's own bound is answered with an error and the model may go on, but the turn's total is the sum over its apps and counts the refused calls too, so a model that keeps calling past a bound can spend the total and end the turn without its `say`. An app that serves tools only sets `listens = false` and `tools`: no loop runs for it, and its tools join the turns of the other apps, each request in a session of its own under a token for its own scope. Its `call_seconds` (default 30) bounds each such request, a relayed call or an opening, so keep it below the persona's `maxDurationSeconds`; nothing refuses a larger value. A tool name may not be shared with a tools-only app, and at least one app must listen. Then the file needs the `[gateway]` table, which is agents' `AgentConfig` (`backend`, `api_group` and `[gateway.gateway]` with `origin`, `key_file` and, for HTTPS, `ca_file`), and every agent a `persona_file`: the absolute path of its agent file `<name>.md`, whose prompt is the agent's character. What an agent may spend is bounded at the model gateway's budget; the runner sets no limit of its own, and the invitation, its removal and the person's permission to be heard are the switches that stop an agent. The agent file names no tools and no call bound and offers the role `user`, under which a turn runs. The token's `agent_name` claim is the name a line must carry to address the agent. `src/personas/config.py` owns its fields and what is refused at start; the runner stops with the field named, never a secret.

The top-level `history_bytes` (default 32768, a positive integer) bounds the history each agent keeps in memory and tells a view turn; the history lives as long as the runner, so a restart starts it empty, and a written turn and a voice decision are not told it.

The optional `[memory]` table names the database that keeps every line the agents read: `host`, `port`, `dbname`, `user`, `password_file` (the absolute path of the file holding the password), `write_seconds`, the bound on one write or read against a database that answers (default 2, `inf` for none; a connection that goes dark in the middle of a write is given up only after the driver's own cancel wait, about ten seconds more). A large or absent bound has a cost: the write runs inside the listen's deadline, so a database that goes dark then holds that loop until the deadline, the app is backed off as a network failure, the turn already running is cancelled with its answer, and no `store_failed` is logged; a bound from about 40 seconds on carries the same risk. Under `inf`, `personas migrate` hands the driver no connect timeout, so the driver's own default bounds the connect; under any bound, a database that stops answering after the connect holds it. The runner starts also when the database cannot be reached. Its first sweep connects at start and checks the schema there, unless `lines_keep_days` is `inf`, and else the first write or read does; the runner logs `store_failed` with the code `store_unreachable` or `store_timeout` once, answers as it always does, and logs `store_resumed` when a write, read or sweep succeeds again; a schema that is behind ends the runner. `personas migrate` brings the schema to its head and needs the table; when it cannot reach the database or meets a database error it prints `personas: memory:` with the code and exits non-zero; run it before the runner starts and after every release. `personas memory list` prints, tab-separated under a header and with a field quoted when it holds a tab or a line break, one row per visit (agent's client id, app, place, generation, the number of lines kept, and the first and the last time a line of it was first read), never a line's text or a speaker's name; `--agent <client id>` narrows it to one agent. `personas memory erase --agent <client id>` deletes every line that agent keeps, also of an agent no longer configured, and `personas memory erase --all` every line of every agent; it prints the number deleted, 0 included, and keeps the schema. Exactly one of the two is given, once and spelled in full, or the command prints its usage and deletes nothing. Both commands need the table and fail like `personas migrate`; a schema not at its head refuses them. Both run under `write_seconds`, so they can also stop with `store_timeout`. An erase that stops so has deleted nothing, unless the bound ran out while the database was answering the commit, in which case they may be gone; running it again with a larger bound erases what is left and prints that number. An erase removes what was kept up to that moment: an agent in a call keeps that call's window again at its next read, because the app still holds it, so erase for good after the agent has left or with the runner stopped. Reading a window again keeps each line once, with the time of its first read. `today_hours` (default 24, `inf` for every kept line) and `today_bytes` (default 32768) set what a turn is told of the day: the lines of the agent's other visits first read within `today_hours`, visit by visit in the order they happened, each with the minutes since its first line, the speakers' names and his own lines marked; past `today_bytes` of names and text the oldest are left out, and the turn is told that they were. A read that fails or outlasts `write_seconds` is logged as `store_failed` like a write, and the turn answers without the day. `lines_keep_days` (default 30, `inf` for every line kept) and `sweep_minutes` (default 60, `inf` for the first sweep only) set how long a line is kept: the runner removes every line of every agent, also of one no longer configured, that was first read longer ago than `lines_keep_days`, once when it starts and then every `sweep_minutes`, and logs `swept` with the number removed and no content. A keep period shorter than `today_hours` shortens what a turn is told of the day to it. A sweep the database cannot answer is logged as `store_failed` once per outage, with its code, and tried again at the next interval; no line is removed meanwhile. A window the app still holds is kept again at the agent's next read, with that time as the first read of any line a sweep had removed.

The runner opens no port. It removes `ready_file` at start, so a marker left by an earlier run never counts, and creates it the first time any loop's read of its standing answers: the start worked, the realm gave a token and an app accepted it. A refused token, a suspended loop, a network failure or a 5xx does not create it, so a wrong secret or URL shows as not ready. The marker stays afterwards; a later suspension or outage is in the log. If the file cannot be removed or created, the runner exits non-zero naming the path. The platform's readiness probe is `test -e <ready_file>`.

The runner stops on SIGTERM with exit code 0 and needs no init, in the image or the deployment. The deployment supplies the configuration, the persona files included, and the secret files; the image holds none of them.

The commands for working on the runner are in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

## Release it

The image is the runner's release. The operator dispatches the `Release` workflow (`.github/workflows/release.yml`) on `main` with the version as `YYYY.MM.DD.N`, for example `2026.11.08.1`. Quality's gate refuses a commit whose checks are not green, the image is pushed to GHCR with an attestation, and then the tag `v<version>` is created. The image holds no configuration; `PERSONAS_CONFIG` and the secret files come from the deployment. An AI agent dispatches a release only on the operator's explicit yes for that one version. [quality's release path](https://github.com/overnightworks/quality/blob/v0.10.0/docs/release.md) owns the rules.
