# Personas

The home of the operator's AI agents: each with its own name, picture, personality and memory, taking part in the operator's apps the way a person does.

For the operator, who gives each agent its name and rights, and for the people who meet his agents in his apps.

## What runs now

The runner. Each agent signs in to the realm as itself, with its own client, and for every app it may reach that listens it keeps one subscription open on the app's MCP endpoint and reads its standing whenever the app says something changed. When it is invited to a call it accepts, and it appears there under the name the realm gives it, with the AI mark. It never leaves on its own; it is removed with its seat, its lease or its client. In a call it reads every line from its join on, takes what was said before a restart or during a pause as read, and every line said after by someone else, unless the app marked it as an act, gives him one model turn through the operator's LiteLLM gateway, in which he decides whether to say something or stay quiet; his own lines give none. With a `[memory]` table it keeps every line it reads, verbatim, in a Postgres of its own, where a turn can look up what was said earlier in its place.

Every turn, written or at a view, is sent the persona and then these user messages, none of them an instruction: a header, one message for each earlier turn of his that was answered while the present still holds it, and the turn's own. The header is his name (`you`) and whether older entries have left his history (`older_dropped`). A turn's message is when the turn was admitted (`at`, UTC to the whole second), what woke him (`woke`) and every entry (`entries`) he took since the message before it, told once, in the order he took them. An entry is from his history (every line he took in, every table state he read, every call relayed for him and every sentence he spoke through `speak` at any of his places since the runner started, in the order he took them, each with its app and the time he took it (`at`, UTC to the whole second), and a line or a table state with its place; a line with its `seq`, his own lines marked, whether it was said aloud (`spoken`) and, told only when it was, that the app cut it before its end (`cut`, its text the part heard, or the whole sentence when the app cannot tell how far playback reached) and, told only when it is one, that it says what someone did rather than what someone said (`act`), a table state with its `revision`, whether it waits for him and the state as the app sent it, a call with its `tool`, the `arguments` he sent and its `outcome`, the app's answer (`"answered": true` with its `text` and whether it `is_error`) or that the app did not answer (`"answered": false` with a `code`), a spoken sentence with its `text` and its `outcome`, that the app took it (`"answered": true`, with `refused` and its code when the app refused it, or with `written` when the app wrote the sentence to the place instead of voicing it) or did not answer (`"answered": false` with a `code`)). `woke` names its app and place and why: at a written place `lines` with the `seqs` of the lines the turn is for that his history still holds, whether older lines were missed and whether his answer is heard aloud, at a view `view` with the `revision` it is for. A turn that ends without an answer (the gateway refuses or fails it, it is cancelled) adds no message, so its entries are told in the message of the next turn. Every message before the turn's own is byte for byte as the turn before it sent it, so the gateway's cache reads it back; a table state is told whole, as the app sent it, in the message of the turn that first held it. What a call held before a restart or what was said in it during a pause is told to no turn unasked, and with a `[memory]` a turn can look it up once it lies before his history's first entry.

In an app that declares `speak`, an agent whose entry has a `[agents.voice]` table may speak in its calls. A line there makes a turn due like any line not his own (a line marked `act` makes none, there either), and the turn is told that its answer is heard aloud (`"aloud": true` in `woke`). The turn holds the same tools as any turn there, `say` included, so it may speak, write, act or stay silent. The text it writes is spoken sentence by sentence through the app's `speak`, to at most what is left of the turn's deadline. The app answers a sentence when its turn to play comes, so the next sentence goes out as soon as the one before is answered, while that one plays, and the app renders it meanwhile; he is never more than one sentence ahead, which is fixed and no setting. The app appends his own spoken line for each sentence as it ends, whole or cut, so his history holds each sentence and its line in order; a tool call goes out and the turn ends only after that line of every sentence the app took has entered, also after a refusal ended the speech. A round that opens with `[silent]` says nothing; a final round with no text and no call ends the turn as a provider error, so the characters end a silent turn with `[silent]`. Each sentence enters his history as a spoken entry. When the last window of a place states that his voice is unavailable there (`voice_state`), the turn there is a written turn: it is not told that its answer is heard aloud, its text is not spoken and it answers through `say`. A refused sentence (`stopped` too, which cuts the sentence playing and drops the rest) ends the turn's speech without a wait for a line of its own, is logged as `speak_refused` with the app's code and is never written by the runner; the turn goes on. A sentence the app answers as written to the place instead is entered as written, without a wait, and his speech goes on. A `speak` that fails as a call ends the turn like a failed call. The persona's `maxDurationSeconds` bounds the turn with its speech: past it no sentence or call goes out and the turn is logged as `turn_ended limit_exceeded`; so it is when an open `speak` has not been answered or his line for a sentence has not entered by then, and the rest of that turn's speech goes. The turn ends once his line for its last sentence has entered, so his next turn, in any app, follows his speech. A persona file with a `voice` section is refused at start. `speak` is the runner's own: naming it in an app's `tools` is refused at start, and it counts against no app's call bound.

A tool result of any app may carry one still picture, an `image/jpeg` block that the library's wire accepts: the model sees it in that turn, in each of the turn's rounds, and his history tells the act with `"image": true` and keeps no picture byte anywhere. An image block of another media type, a second picture or data that is no JPEG breaks the wire and is an unusable answer (the place's app suspends its loop, another app fails as any app does); audio and resource blocks are ignored.

A turn that ends with the model's final answer is logged as `turn_ended` with no code and, when the gateway reported them for every model round, the turn's tokens: `input` (all the model read, cached included), `cache_read`, `cache_write` and `output`, each the sum over the turn's rounds, and `requests`, the number of model requests the turn made (a turn with one tool round makes 2). When a round reported none, the five fields are absent, never zero. A turn that sent a model request also says the size of its input in bytes, by part: `tools_bytes` (the tool declarations each request carries, the lookup's included), `told_bytes` (the told messages, all of them), `results_bytes` (the tool results relayed back to the model, held calls and lookups included; 0 for a turn with no call) and `images_bytes` (the decoded bytes of the pictures those results carried, counted apart from `results_bytes`; 0 without one), counts only and never a name, text or picture. A picture an app answers rides every later model round of its turn, so `input` carries it once per round. A turn that ends short is logged with its code and no token counts, and with the four sizes unless it ended before a request was sent or was cancelled. The counts change nothing the runner does.

A place whose state is no conversation, a table for instance, is served through a view.
The app sets `serves_view` on the agent's standing there, whatever the place's kind, and the agent reads that place through its view and its window.
The view holds the place's newest state: a `revision`, whether the place waits for him, and the app's own `state`.
The window holds what happened there, as numbered lines, and the app lists it beside the view.
An invitation to a place of another kind than a call is accepted like a call's, whether or not its standing serves a view yet (an app marks an invited standing as serving none until it is active), but only in an app where the agent can take turns (a persona, the gateway and bound tools), because a seated agent that cannot act leaves the place waiting; an invitation left out for that reason is logged once with the place's kind and the code `no_turns`.
Only apps the operator configured to listen can invite the agent, and what people put into a view reaches him as their lines do.
After every read of the standing, the agent reads the views of his active view places in the standing's order, then the windows of his active calls and view places.
A window is read like a call's: after his own accept from `seq` 1, a restart or a pause takes what stands as read, and a place that stops serving a view and serves one again starts as restarted.
The new lines of a window enter his history before that wake's view and are told at his next turn; they make no turn due at a place with a view, and a line marked `act` makes none at any place.
A line there can be newer than the view's revision; it is told before the view.
Each view that waits for him gets one model turn.
An app that lists no window beside a view, or answers its read as unknown, costs him no turn and no failure: the standing is read once more, and the view's turn is unchanged.
The turn is told the message every turn is told, above: its message holds the entries he took since the message before it, and `woke` names its app, place and the `revision` it is for.
A line is part of his history whether or not it names him, and what he took from a place stays in it when he leaves the place.
Every call the runner relays for him, to the place's app, to another app where he holds a place or to a tools-only app, enters his history when the app answers or the relay ends without an answer (`"answered": false` with the failure's code, or `cancelled` when the turn was cut while the call was in flight, because its place left or its time ran out): the app may have acted on it, which his own line read back or the table's next state then shows. A call past an app's bound was never sent and does not enter. No call is kept in the store.
So while he is in a call and sits at a table, a turn at either can answer in the call and act at the table, also while it is not his move; the table refuses what its rules do not allow. In a spoken turn a call goes out after the sentences before it. A call to another app goes in a session of its own under a token for that app's scope; when it fails, the model sees an error result, that app takes no more calls in that turn, and one `held_app_failed` (a tools-only app: `tools_only_app_failed`) is logged with its code; a failed call to the turn's own app still ends the turn. Every model round of such a turn carries the declarations of every app it holds, in the configuration's order.
Every revision he reads of a view is an entry of his history too, once however often he reads it, so in a call he knows the table, his hand and the game as far back as his history reaches; what he says of it is his persona's.
No table state is kept in the store, but a table's lines are kept like a call's: after a restart he knows a table's state from its first read, and what was said there before is told to no turn.
It has no instruction; what to do at a table is the persona's to say.
The turn holds the bound tools of its app that the app lists when the turn starts (one it does not list is left out, none is no failure), of every other app where he holds an active place when the turn starts, and of the tools-only apps, and it ends with its place, as a written turn does.
A waiting revision gets one turn, and no second one at that revision, however the turn ended: with a tool call that moves nothing, with no call, with a refused call or with a failed relay; a turn that ends without an answer is retried at its place, as described below.
The runner does not ask again by itself; an app that wants him to act again moves the revision.
A tool's error result reaches the model inside the same turn.
The agent then waits for the app's next revision.
An app therefore moves the revision with every answer of the agent, a declined word included.
The state reaches no log line.

An agent takes one turn at a time across all his apps. What arrives while a turn runs is read and taken into his history at once, and each place with something for him (a line of someone else's, a table waiting for him) gets one turn after it, the place that has waited longest first, told everything that arrived. Before the running turn's next tool call goes out, once per turn, anything not his own that arrived since the turn began (a line of someone else's, an act line or a line at a table included, any table state, the one his own card caused included) holds that call: it is not sent, and the model is told so and what arrived, and decides again; the next message tells those arrivals again. Every line he hears costs a model call, also when he stays quiet, unless it is an act or was said at a place with a view; in a lively call he thinks almost without a pause, and two agents in one call can keep each other talking. The gateway's budget is the only limit.

A turn that ends without an answer (`turn_ended` with a code: the gateway refuses or fails it, a limit is exceeded) pauses his turns at every app: no turn of his starts for a wait of 1 second that doubles with each such turn in a row, up to `budget_pause_cap_seconds` when the gateway refused for a spent budget (`budget_spent`) and up to 30 seconds otherwise, logged once per pause as `paused` with the turn's code and `seconds`. What arrives meanwhile is still taken into his history. After the wait the turn is retried at its place: the place is due again with what the turn was for, so its lines join those that arrived meanwhile in one turn and a view revision waits again, unless a newer view revision made the place due meanwhile. A place he left meanwhile gets no turn, a turn cancelled by his leaving it or by a shutdown is not retried, and the wait holds the turns of his other places just the same. The first turn that ends with an answer ends the pause and starts the wait over.

Which apps an agent can use is the realm's group membership, not this configuration. When the realm or an app refuses an agent (a disabled client, a missing group), that agent's loop for that app is suspended and logged once, and it resumes when the refusal ends, without a restart.

## Run it

Install with `uv sync`, point `PERSONAS_CONFIG` at a TOML file and start `personas`. The file holds the realm's token endpoint, the absolute path `ready_file`, the apps (MCP URL and scope) and the agents (client id and the path of the file holding its client secret). An app that lets its agents answer also lists the tools their turns may call as `tools`, with `max_tool_calls`, the most calls one turn makes in that app; the two are set together. A call past an app's own bound is answered with an error and the model may go on, but the turn's total is the sum over its apps plus one, for the call the turn may hold back, and counts the refused and the held calls too, so a model that keeps calling past a bound can spend the total and end the turn without its `say`. An app that serves tools only sets `listens = false` and `tools`: no loop runs for it, and its tools join the turns of the other apps, each request in a session of its own under a token for its own scope. Its `call_seconds` (default 30) bounds each such request, a relayed call or an opening, so keep it below the persona's `maxDurationSeconds`; nothing refuses a larger value. A tool name may be bound by one app only, since one turn holds the tools of several, and at least one app must listen. No app binds `speak` or `recall`. With a `[memory]` table every turn also holds the lookup `recall`, first among its tools (see `[memory]`); its `lookup_calls` add to the turn's total, which is then the sum over the apps plus `lookup_calls` plus one, or `lookup_calls` plus one for a turn whose apps hold no tool, and no call is refused or held back in any app because the turn looked. An app that listens may set `call_seconds` too; it bounds each request a turn of another app makes to it. A turn declares the tools of its apps in the order of the file, so list the apps whose places come and go last. Then the file needs the `[gateway]` table, which is agents' `AgentConfig` (`backend`, `api_group` and `[gateway.gateway]` with `origin`, `key_file` and, for HTTPS, `ca_file`), and every agent a `persona_file`: the absolute path of its agent file `<name>.md`, whose prompt is the agent's character. An agent may also set `model`, a `text/<model>` gateway model name that his turns run on instead of the agent file's `model`, so a switch needs a configuration change and a restart, not a release; the start logs each agent's model, and a name that is no text model stops the start. The image holds each agent's file this repository ships at `/app/characters/<name>.md`, from `src/characters/`; a deployment names it there, so a character changes with a release. An agent's figure file `src/characters/<name>.vrm` is written by `scripts/write_figures.py <directory>` and never by hand; the runner does not read it, the image does not hold it, and the `Figures` job fails on a file the generator does not write and runs figure's check on each. The same command writes an agent's room `src/characters/<name>.room.glb`, the static scene his figure stands in, which the `Figures` job checks as a room; no release publishes it yet. What an agent may spend is bounded at the model gateway's budget; the runner sets no limit of its own, and the invitation, its removal and the person's permission to be heard are the switches that stop an agent. The agent file names no tools and no call bound and offers the role `user`, under which a turn runs. An agent may also have a `[agents.figure]` table with `url` (an absolute `https` address without user part, query or fragment) and `sha256` (64 lowercase hexadecimal characters of the figure's bytes); every accept of that agent, at a call or at a table, in every app, carries it, and an app that shows figures and allows that address shows him with it. An agent without one is accepted as before and the app shows its default. An agent may also have a `[agents.voice]` table: its presence turns the agent's spoken turns on at every call place of an app that declares `speak`, and its optional `names` is an ordered list of speech service voice ids as they stand, 1 to 8 and none twice, the rule of agents' `Voices` type (for example `["de_DE-thorsten-medium", "Rachel_V2.en-GB"]`), first choice first. Every accept of that agent carries that order as `voices`, and the speech client takes the first one the app offers; the runner picks and reorders none. Without `names` the accept carries no `voices` and the app's default voice speaks. A malformed list, an empty one, a repeated voice, more than 8 and a `name` key stop the start with the field named. The runner checks neither the names against what the app offers nor the app's wish to speak. The runner fetches nothing and checks nothing about the bytes or the address; changing a figure needs a restart. The token's `agent_name` claim is the name a line must carry to address the agent. `src/personas/config.py` owns its fields and what is refused at start; the runner stops with the field named, never a secret.

The top-level `budget_pause_cap_seconds` (default 300, a number of at least 1) is the longest wait between an agent's turns while the gateway refuses them for a spent budget.

The top-level `history_bytes` (default 49152, a positive integer) bounds the history each agent keeps in memory and tells every turn, and `history_keep_bytes` (default 32768, a positive integer, refused at start when above `history_bytes`) is what a step leaves: both count a line, a call and a spoken sentence by the bytes of the JSON each is told as, and a table state by its JSON bytes. Between steps the history only grows, so every request is its earlier one plus what is new and the gateway reads the earlier messages from its cache. Past `history_bytes` the oldest whole messages leave in one step until what stays fits `history_keep_bytes`, the header says that older entries left, and the next request is written to the cache anew; the newest message always stays, and when it alone is past `history_keep_bytes` its oldest entries leave too, but its newest entry always stays, so the history may exceed `history_bytes` by that one entry. At the default a skat game (40 to 50 states of about 1.5 KB) fills the history after about half a game, and what was said in the call before the step is no longer in it and reaches no turn. A larger value keeps more and makes every request longer; the history lives as long as the runner, so a restart starts it empty.

The optional `[memory]` table names the database that keeps every line the agents read: `host`, `port`, `dbname`, `user`, `password_file` (the absolute path of the file holding the password), `write_seconds`, the bound on one write or read against a database that answers (default 2, `inf` for none; a connection that goes dark in the middle of a write is given up only after the driver's own cancel wait, about ten seconds more). A large or absent bound has a cost: the write runs inside the listen's deadline, so a database that goes dark then holds that loop until the deadline, the app is backed off as a network failure, the turn already running is cancelled with its answer, and no `store_failed` is logged; a bound from about 40 seconds on carries the same risk. Under `inf`, `personas migrate` hands the driver no connect timeout, so the driver's own default bounds the connect; under any bound, a database that stops answering after the connect holds it. The runner starts also when the database cannot be reached. Its first sweep connects at start and checks the schema there, unless `lines_keep_days` is `inf`, and else the first write or read does; the runner logs `store_failed` with the code `store_unreachable` or `store_timeout` once, answers as it always does, and logs `store_resumed` when a write, read or sweep succeeds again; a schema that is behind ends the runner. `personas migrate` brings the schema to its head and needs the table; when it cannot reach the database or meets a database error it prints `personas: memory:` with the code and exits non-zero; run it before the runner starts and after every release. `personas memory list` prints, tab-separated under a header and with a field quoted when it holds a tab or a line break, one row per visit (agent's client id, app, place, generation, the number of lines kept, and the first and the last time a line of it was first read), never a line's text or a speaker's name; `--agent <client id>` narrows it to one agent. `personas memory erase --agent <client id>` deletes every line that agent keeps, also of an agent no longer configured, and `personas memory erase --all` every line of every agent; it prints the number deleted, 0 included, and keeps the schema. Exactly one of the two is given, once and spelled in full, or the command prints its usage and deletes nothing. Both commands need the table and fail like `personas migrate`; a schema not at its head refuses them. Both run under `write_seconds`, so they can also stop with `store_timeout`. An erase that stops so has deleted nothing, unless the bound ran out while the database was answering the commit, in which case they may be gone; running it again with a larger bound erases what is left and prints that number. An erase removes what was kept up to that moment: an agent in a call keeps that call's window again at its next read, because the app still holds it, so erase for good after the agent has left or with the runner stopped. Reading a window again keeps each line once, with the time of its first read. `lines_keep_days` (default 30, `inf` for every line kept) and `sweep_minutes` (default 60, `inf` for the first sweep only) set how long a line is kept: the runner removes every line of every agent, also of one no longer configured, that was first read longer ago than `lines_keep_days`, once when it starts and then every `sweep_minutes`, and logs `swept` with the number removed and no content. A sweep the database cannot answer is logged as `store_failed` once per outage, with its code, and tried again at the next interval; no line is removed meanwhile. A window the app still holds is kept again at the agent's next read, with that time as the first read of any line a sweep had removed.

With a `[memory]` table a turn, written, aloud or at a view, can look up what was said earlier in its own place. The lookup `recall` takes one string, `text`, and reads only the lines this agent kept in the turn's app and place, every visit of it; no argument names another place, app or agent. A line matches when its text holds every word of `text` (Postgres full text, the `simple` configuration: no stemming, so `foxes` does not find `fox`), and a `text` without a word matches every line. Only lines first read before the oldest entry the turn's history still holds are given, since the history tells the rest; so right after a pause a line said in it is found once it is older than that entry. Lines kept while his place was paused, or held by a window at a restart, are told to no turn unasked and are found like any other. The result holds its scope (`this room, before your history`), the matching lines in the history's line form, newest first, as many as fit `lookup_bytes` and never more than the wire carries in one result, and `older_dropped` when more matched. `lookup_calls` (default 2) is how many lookups one turn makes and `lookup_bytes` (default 8192) bounds one result and is the longest `text` a lookup accepts; each is an integer, `lookup_calls` above 0 and `lookup_bytes` at least what a result without a line takes, and 0, a smaller number, a fraction or a string refuses the start; no value turns the lookup off (removing the `[memory]` table does, and stops keeping lines too). A further lookup is refused with an error of its own, a `text` that is missing, no string or too long is an error result without a read, and a lookup is never held back for what arrived and takes nothing into his history, so every earlier message stays as it was sent. The read is bounded by `write_seconds` and has no index. A database that cannot answer gives the model an error result and `store_failed` once per outage, and the turn goes on and answers; a room too large for `write_seconds` shows the same way although the database is up. Any other database error, and a schema behind its head, end the runner as for a write. A lookup reaches back `lines_keep_days`, plus at most the wait for the next sweep; a line a sweep removed from a window the app still holds is kept again with a new first-read time and is found with that later time. What a lookup finds can reach any app in the same turn, and across visits of one room it gives lines a listener present now may not have heard. A lookup adds no log event and no content to the log; it shows in `turn_ended` as `results_bytes` and `tools_bytes`.

The runner opens no port. It removes `ready_file` at start, so a marker left by an earlier run never counts, and creates it the first time any loop's read of its standing answers: the start worked, the realm gave a token and an app accepted it. A refused token, a suspended loop, a network failure or a 5xx does not create it, so a wrong secret or URL shows as not ready. The marker stays afterwards; a later suspension or outage is in the log. If the file cannot be removed or created, the runner exits non-zero naming the path. The platform's readiness probe is `test -e <ready_file>`.

The runner stops on SIGTERM with exit code 0 and needs no init, in the image or the deployment. The deployment supplies the configuration and the secret files; the image holds neither, only the agents' files.

The commands for working on the runner are in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

## Release it

The image is the runner's release. Once `main`'s CI is green, an agent dispatches the `Release` workflow (`.github/workflows/release.yml`) on `main` with the version as `YYYY.MM.DD.N`, for example `2026.11.08.1`. Quality's gate refuses a commit whose checks are not green, the image is pushed to GHCR with an attestation, and then the tag `v<version>` is created with the agents' figure files `src/characters/*.vrm` as the release's assets. The release notes open with one `figure` line per agent (`<name>: figure = { url = ..., sha256 = ... }`: the file's address in that release and the sha256 of its bytes, written by `scripts/figure_release_notes.sh` in the same run), to paste, without the name and its colon, into that agent's `[[agents]]` entry of the runner's TOML (it is the same as that entry's `[agents.figure]` table). An app that shows figures fetches them only from the prefix its platform allows (hopin's setting; `https://github.com/overnightworks/personas/releases/download/` is accepted). The image holds no runner configuration (`PERSONAS_CONFIG`); it holds the agents' files, so a change of a character is released like code. `PERSONAS_CONFIG` and the secret files come from the deployment. [quality's release path](https://github.com/overnightworks/quality/blob/v0.12.0/docs/release.md) owns the rules.
